# FAMILY_PERMISSION_MODEL.md

# SafeHome Family Permission & Access Model

Version: MVP → Multi-Family Expansion Phase

Purpose:
Define family structure, user roles, permissions, invitation systems, elder ownership boundaries, and access governance for SafeHome.

These rules apply to:
- family accounts
- shared dashboards
- caregivers
- future installers
- future admin systems
- notification routing
- elder monitoring permissions

---

# CORE PRINCIPLE

Elder safety should remain collaborative,
while elder dignity and privacy remain protected.

Family access should feel:
- supportive
- trustworthy
- calm
- permission-based

NOT:
- surveillance-heavy
- invasive
- confusing
- authority-abusive

---

# FAMILY STRUCTURE MODEL

Future SafeHome structure:

Family
 ├── Primary Owner
 ├── Family Members
 ├── Caregivers
 ├── Emergency Contacts
 └── Elders

Each elder belongs to:
- one primary family account

Each family may eventually support:
- multiple elders
- multiple family members
- multiple caregivers

---

# ROLE TYPES

## 1. Primary Owner

The Primary Owner is:
- the main account creator
- the family authority owner
- the billing owner (future)
- the escalation configuration owner

Primary Owner permissions:
- invite family members
- remove family members
- configure alerts
- configure escalation rules
- manage caregivers
- configure notification preferences
- export family data
- delete family accounts
- manage elders

Primary Owner responsibilities:
- maintain trusted access
- verify invited users
- protect elder privacy

There should only be:
ONE Primary Owner per family account.

---

## 2. Family Member

Family Members are:
- adult children
- spouses
- siblings
- trusted relatives

Family Member permissions:
- view dashboard
- receive alerts
- receive summaries
- participate in check-ins
- view elder activity summaries

Family Members MUST NOT:
- delete family accounts
- modify critical escalation rules
- remove Primary Owner
- access internal system settings

Family Members should support:
family reassurance,
NOT system administration.

---

## 3. Caregiver

Caregivers may include:
- home care workers
- assisted living staff
- trusted helpers

Caregiver permissions should be limited.

Recommended permissions:
- receive selected alerts
- view simplified dashboard
- acknowledge notifications
- submit status updates

Caregivers SHOULD NOT:
- export family data
- manage billing
- change family structure
- access sensitive account settings

Caregiver access should remain:
task-oriented.

---

## 4. Emergency Contact

Emergency Contacts may:
- receive emergency escalation notifications
- receive urgent alerts

Emergency Contacts SHOULD NOT:
- access dashboard history
- access configuration settings
- access family management

Emergency access should remain:
minimal and purpose-specific.

---

## 5. Future Installer / Support Roles

Future installer roles may include:
- hardware setup
- onboarding assistance
- device troubleshooting

Installers MUST NEVER:
- access behavioral history unnecessarily
- access passwords
- access family communications
- access billing systems

Installer access should remain:
temporary and scoped.

---

# ELDER OWNERSHIP MODEL

Each elder should belong to:
ONE family account structure.

Future systems may allow:
- shared caregiver access
- shared emergency contact access

However:
elder ownership boundaries must remain clear.

---

# INVITATION SYSTEM RULES

Invitations should:
- require approval
- use expiring invite tokens
- clearly explain permissions
- preserve auditability

Future invitation flows should support:
- family invite emails
- caregiver invites
- emergency contact invites

Invite tokens MUST:
- expire
- be revocable
- never appear in logs

---

# ACCESS CONTROL PRINCIPLES

Users should only access:
- authorized elders
- authorized dashboards
- permitted notification settings

No user should gain access:
simply by knowing an email address.

All protected routes MUST require:
- authentication
- permission validation
- family ownership validation

---

# MULTI-ELDER FUTURE DIRECTION

Future architecture may support:

Family
 ├── Elder A
 ├── Elder B
 └── Elder C

Examples:
- parents
- grandparents
- assisted living rooms

Permission systems should support:
elder-level visibility controls.

---

# ALERT PERMISSION MODEL

Not all users should receive all alerts.

Future systems should support:
- alert routing preferences
- escalation preferences
- quiet hours
- caregiver-only alerts
- emergency-only escalation

The goal is:
reducing alert fatigue.

---

# DATA VISIBILITY RULES

Different roles should see different levels of detail.

Example:

Primary Owner:
- full dashboard
- escalation settings
- notification history

Family Member:
- daily summaries
- alerts
- reassurance dashboard

Caregiver:
- operational alerts only

Emergency Contact:
- emergency notifications only

---

# AI ACCESS CONTROL RULES

AI systems MUST respect:
- family permissions
- elder ownership
- role visibility boundaries

AI MUST NEVER:
- expose hidden elder data
- expose unauthorized alerts
- bypass role restrictions

---

# AUDITABILITY PRINCIPLE

Future systems should support:
- invitation history
- permission changes
- alert acknowledgements
- escalation history

Auditability improves:
- trust
- accountability
- operational clarity

---

# FAMILY EXPERIENCE PHILOSOPHY

SafeHome should feel:
- collaborative
- reassuring
- emotionally stable
- respectful

NOT:
- controlling
- invasive
- authority-heavy

Families should feel:
supported,
not monitored.

---

# LONG-TERM DIRECTION

Future systems may support:
- family discussion workflows

- caregiver coordination
- AI-assisted collaboration

human relationships remain primary.

AI remains:
an assistant layer.

---

Family trust is more important than feature complexity.

Permissions exist to protect:
- privacy
- dignity
- trust
- clarity
- emotional safety
